The SEED Laboratory studies why children and adolescents with shared familial liability for psychosis and bipolar spectrum disorders follow different developmental trajectories — and how to recognize those signatures early.

The central question

Why do some young people with shared familial liability progress to serious mental illness — while others remain resilient?

We look for the earliest developmental paraphenotypes: traits that emerge alongside clinical symptoms from shared neurodevelopmental systems. Finding them early could show which path a child is on, years before illness takes hold.

Our work focuses on childhood and adolescence, with the goal of scalable, clinically useful tools for earlier identification, more personalized care, and preemptive psychiatry.

The name

What SEED stands for

Each word names part of what we look for: measurable traits that appear early and help tell paths apart.

S

Signatures

Measurable traits that emerge alongside clinical symptoms from shared neurodevelopmental systems.

E

Early

Childhood and adolescence, when developmental processes shape later outcomes.

E

Emergence

The first appearance of traits and symptoms, long before a diagnosis is made.

D

Divergence

How young people who share familial liability come to follow different trajectories.
